# Adventure 1: The Traitor's Gambit
*Act 1 · Adventure 1*

**In-fiction start:** Primeday, 4 Elona, Year 5
*Equivalent to 4 Elona, 7982 C.R.C. (15 BBY). Default day index 1475.*

## Summary
The heroes are drawn into a cantina confrontation on Jakku, discover a stolen Imperial code cylinder, use it to download the coordinates of a secret prison, and break Admiral Varth out of a jungle fortress — all for the promise of a stolen fortune.

## Story Marks (Achievements)
- **The Burning Deck** — The Burning Deck is in chaos — Maya's enforcers are cornering her at the bar. End the confrontation.
- **The Broker** — Switch has what you need: the where and the when of the man worth a fortune. He doesn't give it up easily.
- **Defend the Sinkhole** — Ganga Lor's enforcers crashed the bunker — they tracked you here for revenge. Hold the line. Switch doesn't fight his own battles, but he remembers who fought for him.
- **Ghost in the Machine** — Slip the Code Cylinder into the Imperial shuttle's nav core. Find where the Empire is moving the man worth a fortune.
- **Into the Green Hell** — Get through the jungle and reach Mandrake's compound.
- **The Crash** — V-wings strafed the Banshee out of the sky. Salvage the ship and what's in it — or leave it dead in the canopy. The crew that flies again does so because someone's hands kept her flying.
- **The Vanishing Place** — Get inside the fortress without lighting it up.
- **Mandrake's Bargain** — A Separatist holdout offers a way inside the fortress to free the Klosari who took him in — if you'll sabotage the generator on the way through. Decide what owing him is worth.
- **Vischera** — Dr. Vischera is six months from a working cure for an Outer Rim plague — built on the bodies of Klosari villagers Skarn supplies her. She is unarmed and unrepentant. Decide what she is worth.
- **The Generator** — The fortress runs on one heart. Stop it beating — or learn what it's hiding.
- **Free the Block** — Skarn's holding cells are full of Klosari prisoners — Mandrake's adopted people, penned behind the fortress's laser fences. Open them, or leave them sealed.
- **The Extraction** — Pull Varth out of the Vanishing Place and break atmosphere. The first stranger you decided was worth more than your own skin. The road that begins here ends at someone else's cell, years from now.
- **The Crucible** — Whatever your plan was, it didn't survive contact. You did.

#### Read Aloud
> Jakku. A world the galaxy forgot... if it ever noticed. Out here on the Western Reaches, the holonet feeds are weeks old, the Republic is a memory, and the Empire is a rumor that sometimes wears a uniform. You've heard the stories filtering through the spacer lanes: whole systems pacified, Separatist holdouts burned out of their bunkers, the clone army quietly replaced by men who volunteer. The Core worlds call it order. Out here, people just call it the new way things are.
> 
> Reestkii squats on the desert flats like something the sand is trying to swallow. Drifters. Smugglers. Moisture farmers with nothing left to farm. Varga the Hutt runs what passes for an economy: debt, protection, and the quiet understanding that Imperial patrols from Arkanis Sector come through maybe twice a standard year... and they never stay.
> 
> You stepped off a rust-bucket passenger transport twelve hours ago — together, however you found each other. Maybe you have history. Maybe this is the first day of it. Either way, you're here for the same reason: a fixer named Cade Ryll put the word out through the spacer lanes — crew needed for a short-haul job, good pay, no questions. He named this cantina. He named a time. That was an hour ago.
> 
> The Burning Deck. Low ceiling. Failing glow-rods. Engine coolant and cheap Corellian whiskey in the air. A Devaronian works a credit chip across his knuckles at the sabacc table — watching, playing nothing. Two Abyssin drifters argue in the corner booth over a rusted motivator. A weathered Togruta woman nurses a drink at the bar, montrals scarred ex-military, hand never far from her hip. Behind the bar, a nervous Bith wipes the same glass he's been wiping for twenty minutes, his eyes flicking to the door every time the wind rattles the blast shutters.
> 
> The Burning Deck is quieter than it should be tonight. You noticed it when you docked: a Lambda-class shuttle, sitting on the landing field, flanked by two stormtroopers standing in the heat like they were carved from plastoid. Nobody docks a Lambda on Jakku without a reason. Fewer faces in the stalls on the walk in. The regulars are nursing their drinks a little harder. Talking a little softer. Something has changed the arithmetic, and nobody knows the new numbers yet.
> 
> Your drinks are warm. Your fixer is an hour late. And nothing on Jakku is going the way it was supposed to.
> 
> The blast doors hiss open, and the heat pours in.
> 
> A young human woman. Dark hair, flight jacket, maybe twenty-five. She half-runs, half-stumbles through the entrance. One hand is pressed against her ribs, where her jacket is scorched through. She's bleeding. The Bith bartender freezes mid-wipe. The Devaronian at the sabacc table looks up for the first time all night.
>

#### GM Notes
TWO-BEAT STRUCTURE: This scene is split into two read-aloud blocks. Read Part 1 (the cantina, the crew waiting, the patrons, the atmosphere) and then PAUSE. Ask 'What do you do while you wait?' Let the players explore the room, talk to each other, interact with patrons, investigate the Imperial shuttle — whatever they want. This is their first moment in the world. Don't rush it. When the energy is right — or when they need a push — read Part 2 (Maya's entrance).

  CADE RYLL — THE TRUTH (GM ONLY, NEVER REVEAL AT THE TABLE): "Cade Ryll" is a cover identity. The man behind the name is a deep-cover Hidden Path operative — a survivor of the Jedi Purge who has spent the last four years building a small underground railroad for Force-sensitive children and Jedi remnants. Among Force-sensitives he is unusually good at reading Shatterpoints — the moments where the smallest push tips an enormous amount of weight — and weeks ago he saw the line that runs from this cantina, through Maya, through a stolen Imperial code cylinder, all the way out to ancient Jedi navigational holocrons he has been hunting for two years. The crew at this table is the only domino he sets in the entire campaign. Everything that follows is supposed to fall on its own. SURFACE PLAY GUIDANCE IS UNCHANGED: he never shows, no NPC arrives in his place, and the meet pivots to Maya. WHY HE IS NOT HERE: two reasons. First, the Lambda on the landing field is two days into a tightening Imperial sweep and his face is on a list — being in this room would get him killed and this crew arrested. Second and more important, his presence at the table would destroy the moral pressure the Shatterpoint needs to land on the heroes themselves; if a Jedi is sitting between them and Maya, they fight for the stranger because the man with the lightsaber said to. He needs them to choose. WHY HE IS AN HOUR LATE: he is not late by accident. He scheduled the meet to be exactly empty by the time Maya arrived, calculated from her last known movement pattern. She took roughly ninety minutes longer than he expected — the only beat he did not see coming. PLANT THE TELL (this matters later): the Devaronian sitting alone in the west alcove turning a credit chip across his knuckles is the watcher's first quiet cameo — the "Siv Keel" identity described below in THE PATRONS is his cover for this scene, not a separate NPC. Make the chip-on-knuckles gesture noticeable in passing — a beat of description, nothing more. He never speaks to the crew, leaves before the fight, and is gone by the time anyone thinks to look for him. From here on, every adventure or two, an unnamed stranger somewhere in the scene will turn a credit chip across their knuckles. Different cover, same hand. The reveal lands long after Adventure 10. If the players ask around for "Cade Ryll," nobody at The Burning Deck knows the name — keep it that way. The full lore for the watcher and the Hidden Path lives in the campaign bible (Major Characters → The Watcher; Faction Tracker → The Hidden Path). Do not narrate any of this. Do not hint at his identity, his Force-sensitivity, or his faction. Let the breadcrumb sit.

  THE PATRONS: The cantina regulars are set dressing with depth if the players engage. The Devaronian (sabacc alcove) is a small-time gambler named Siv Keel — he knows the cantina well and can tell the PCs that the Burning Deck has been tense since the Lambda landed two days ago (Day 2, Month 1, Year 5 — GM clarity only). The Abyssin drifters are arguing over a water claim in the badlands — they're loud but harmless and can point the PCs toward the deep desert if asked. The Togruta at the bar is Nela Bren — an ex-Republic military scout who deserted after Order 66. She won't volunteer much but responds to respect; she can confirm Imperial patrols have increased in the sector. The Bith bartender, Dodd Mull, is terrified — he owes Varga money and is afraid any trouble in his establishment will bring the Hutt's people down on him. If weapons come out, the patrons scatter — most for the front entrance, a few ducking down the back hallway behind the bar.

  MAYA'S BACKSTORY (GM ONLY): Maya intercepted an Imperial Code Cylinder before Varga's operators could get their hands on it. She knew it was valuable — encrypted Imperial data, potentially worth a fortune to the right buyer. She thought she could deal with Varga: cut him in, negotiate a split, walk away clean. But Varga doesn't negotiate over things he already considers his. The cylinder was moving through his supply chain. In his eyes, she stole from him. He sent Krev's crew to recover it. They caught up to her outside the cantina — Krev put a warning shot into her ribs to slow her down. She ran anyway. The foot chase ended at The Burning Deck's blast doors.

  MAYA'S ENTRANCE: This is NOT an assassination scene. The enforcers are a retrieval crew for Varga's operation — they're here to recover the cylinder, not kill Maya. They have NOT drawn weapons inside the cantina. Maya's collision with the heroes' table looks like an accident — she tripped on a chair leg while running wounded. But the GM should know: she was already calculating. She clocked the armed crew at the table on her way in and angled toward them. The trip was real — she's hurt and off-balance — but the direction she ran was deliberate. The players should NOT know this yet. The Insight check (sc-s1-read-maya) is the gate for that information. To the players, she's just a wounded stranger who crashed into their table at the worst possible time.

  MAYA'S DIALOGUE: 'Sorry' and 'Don't move' are deliberately ambiguous. She's not explicitly asking them to play along or be her shield. She's just a hurt person trying to survive the next thirty seconds. The enforcers see her at the crew's table and make the assumption themselves — 'This doesn't have to involve anyone else' implies THEY think there might be a connection. The heroes can choose to confirm or deny that assumption.

  NARRATIVE PACING: This scene runs on narrative pacing. Do NOT call for dice unless failure forks the story. Maya crashing in — narrative. Krev's ultimatum — narrative. The bartender's nervousness — narrative. Only roll when a PC takes an action where failure has real consequences (escalation, loss of initiative, or missed intelligence).

  THE IMPERIAL SHUTTLE: Foreshadowing only. The Lambda on the landing field is not part of this scene — it seeds future tension. If players investigate during the pause, the stormtroopers say nothing. Locals don't know why it's here. Dodd the bartender nervously changes the subject. This pays off later.

  MAYA'S WOUND: Real. A blaster graze to her side — Krev's warning shot outside the cantina to slow her down during the foot chase. Non-lethal but painful and bleeding. She is desperate — genuinely — but she's also smarter than she looks.

  RESOLUTION PATHS:
  • QUICK-DRAW INTIMIDATE: A Gunslinger draws fast — blaster out before the enforcers can react. This IS the Intimidate check (Moderate), not a separate action. The draw is the statement. The blaster is leverage, not escalation. Success: Krev folds ('Not worth it today'). Failure: Krev calls the bluff ('You won't shoot over some girl you don't know') — now the PC must decide: pull the trigger (firefight) or holster (Krev smells weakness, uses Lean In).
  • SOCIAL: Charm, Deception, or Persuasion (Moderate) can convince the enforcers Maya isn't worth the trouble, redirect them, or negotiate a delay.
  • BRAWL: If anyone throws a punch, the Kyuzo respond in kind. Bar brawl — fists, headbutts, bottles. Stun damage. Blasters stay holstered.
  • FIREFIGHT: Only triggers if (a) a brawl is already happening AND someone draws a blaster, OR (b) the social check fails AND a PC with blaster already drawn refuses to hand Maya over. A drawn blaster alone is not an escalation — it's a negotiating tool. This is Star Wars.

  IF COMBAT TRIGGERS — [Join Battle] Reference:
  • Control Step-Down: Highest enemy Tier = 1 → step down Control Die 1 time
  • Failed Control (1-3): PC is surprised — [Disoriented] + [Exposed] until end of first turn
  • Mastery (8+): PC designates one enemy as surprised — [Disoriented] + [Exposed] on that NPC
  • Power Die result = initiative slot (higher acts first). NPCs act at the static Initiative shown on their stat block
  • NPC static initiative: Krev = 5, Kyuzo = 5

  Scene Tactics (NPC-agnostic — survives any NPC swap):
  • Hostiles enter through the front entrance. Expect a heavy/anchor type to push the PCs' table and lock down the social-pressure space; lighter/faster bodies will try to flank along the booth row and the alcove.
  • The center floor is the kill zone — no cover, clear sightlines. The booth row and the alcove offer light cover. The bar counter is hard cover with improvised weapons (bottles, glasses) within reach.
  • The back hallway behind the bar is the only secondary route out. The store room is a trap — full cover, no exit.
  • Door controls at the front entrance can be slammed to cut off reinforcements OR to trap the PCs inside (GM's call based on stakes).
  • Failing glow-rods make light unreliable — GMs can rule darkness/concealment tactical effects as the brawl moves.
  • Brawl rules: stun damage, improvised weapons, blasters stay holstered. If a blaster comes out, the scene shifts to lethal.
  • Bystanders scatter when weapons appear — most break for the front entrance, a few duck down the back hallway.

  KEY LORE: 15 BBY. Imperial presence on Jakku is thin — Arkanis Sector patrols, no garrison. The Lambda shuttle today is unusual and unsettling. Varga the Hutt runs the real economy here: smuggling, debt, protection. A cantina fight draws local attention (Varga's people, scavenger gangs) but NOT Imperial attention — the troopers at the landing field don't care about Hutt business.

#### Pacing
- **Opening:** Part 1 establishes Jakku, the crew's identity, the missing fixer, and the subdued cantina. Players get time to explore the room and its patrons. The pause gives them ownership of the space before the plot arrives.
- **Rising action:** Part 2 — Maya crashes in wounded. Thugs fill the doorway. Krev delivers his ultimatum. Kyuzo silently flank. The crew must decide whether this stranger's problem is their problem.
- **Climax:** The decision — fast-draw intimidate, social play, or let it go to fists. One roll that matters.
- **Resolution:** The fight is done. The cantina is wrecked. The thugs are gone — one way or another. Maya is still here, bleeding at the table, watching you with an expression that is not quite gratitude.

**Rooms:**
- **The Kitchen** — Heat rolls out of the open serving hatch in waves, carrying the smell of frying nerf sausage and something older — a stew that's been going so long it's become geological. The galley is cramped: two cook-surfaces, one electric, one burning actual fuel with a visible flame. A droid chassis is bolted to the prep counter — an old enforcer model, its arms replaced with cooking implements, cracked photoreceptor tracking the room with mechanical patience. Grease coats every surface. The exhaust hood shudders and rattles overhead, venting smoke through a duct in the back wall. Past the fuel tank, you can just make out what might be a panel in the wall — hard to tell in the dim light and the haze.
- **Utility Closet** — A heavy latch seals the door. Through the gap at the bottom, you can see stacked crates — dozens of them, floor to ceiling. The smell of cheap whiskey seeps out, sharp and sweet. A powered-down mop droid is just visible through the gap, slumped in the corner like it gave up a long time ago. The latch is old but solid. Nobody's invited you back here.
- **The Bar** — A long durasteel counter dominates this side of the room — scarred, burned, gouged by years of hard use. If you lean over far enough, you can make out Imperial Navy serial numbers stamped into the underside. Six mismatched stools sit bolted to the stone floor. Behind the counter, shelves of bottles in every color catch the amber glow-rod light. The carbonite-chilled tap system breathes cold air across the top of the bar; condensation beads on the fittings and drips onto the durasteel. The smell of spilled whiskey is ground into the surface like varnish. A glass clinks somewhere in the back. The bartender stands behind it all, wiping the same glass in slow circles, eyes flicking to the blast doors every few seconds. At the far end of the counter, a lone figure sits on the last stool, back to the room, facing the wall. She has been nursing the same drink for a long time. Her right hand has not moved far from her hip all night.
- **Private Booth — West Alcove** — The darkest corner of the cantina. The nearest glow-rod is dead, and the only light comes from the faint amber bleed of the dining floor. A beaded partition hangs where a curtain used to be, clicking softly when the ceiling fan pushes the air. The kitchen heat seeps through the stone wall behind the booth, making this alcove uncomfortably warm. Past the curtain, the booth sits empty in the half-dark — a table, two seats, a burned-out glow-rod that nobody has replaced. The air in here is stale and still.
- **Private Booth — Center-West** — A smaller booth, functional, worn down to its bones. - **Private Booth — Center-East** — You hear this booth before you see it — deep, guttural voices that carry across the cantina noise even at a low rumble. #### GM Notes

He learned their language. He wears their craftsmanship. He is theirs now.

=== ACT 1 — THE ENCOUNTER (Jungle) ===
THE FIRST EXCHANGE IS A TEST: He doesn't introduce himself. Then the rifle lowers (not all the way — to his hip) and he says: "Come on. I want you to see something." He turns and starts walking uphill without looking back.

=== ACT 2 — THE RIDGE (Pitch) ===
He leads them up a steep game trail for roughly twenty minutes — climbing hard, through tangled growth, past blind-spots he's used for years. The trail breaks at a ridge above the canopy. THIS is where the crew gets their first look at the Vanishing Place.

THE VIEW: A moss-covered temple ruin in the valley below, fortified with scavenged Clone Wars hardware. Dark basalt walls. Laser fences glowing red along the perimeter, visible through the canopy gaps. Two gun turret emplacements on the east and west walls, servos audible as a faint grinding in the quiet. A Lambda-class shuttle on the rooftop landing pad — a pristine Imperial ship on an ancient stone platform. A squad of white-armored Stormtroopers at parade rest outside the west wall, a jarring contrast to the grimy mercenary outlines in the windows.

Mandrake sits against a lichen-covered stone at the ridge edge, rifle across his knees. NOW he talks. This is where the structured conversation hook fires — and where the pitch lands, because the target is in full view below.

CONVERSATION HOOK — `mandrake-the-deal` (data/conversations/mandrake-the-deal.json): Comfort starts at 3 (guarded but cooperative). Story topics about the Klosari, the jungle, or his old unit hold or raise comfort; business topics about the generator, sluice route, and charge spec are neutral; probe topics about his loyalty, past, or what happens to him after the deal cost 1 comfort each. Acceptance closes the conversation cleanly and opens the descent to the sluice; comfort exhaustion ends it with the offer withdrawn and the ridge view as the last thing the crew shares with him.

THE REVEAL, IN PIECES — let the conversation deliver these naturally, and POINT at the target as you narrate:
1. THE PRISONERS: Klosari resistors are inside the fortress, penned behind the laser fences the crew can see glowing through the gaps. Mandrake has been planning to free them for months but doesn't have the firepower alone.
2. THE DEAL: He'll guide the crew in through an old drainage sluice — the outer grate sits in a hollow on the jungle floor several meters out from the fortress's northeast face, with a stagnant-water underground crawl that runs back under the wall and surfaces inside the perimeter (point — the crew can just make out the moss on the sunken grate from here). One sabotage. Four problems solved. With the target in view. The plan clicks at the table, not in your notes.

It is tense but not mechanically demanding; narrate local flavor (Klosari trail markers, scavenged commando cache points, a boobytrap he disarms without comment). The descent ends in a hollow several meters out from the fortress's northeast face — moss, dead leaves, and a sunken grate in the jungle floor. He works it loose with a vibroblade. He steps back into the canopy and is gone — the ridge view is yours now, and only yours. The sluice grate is still down there. The crew can find it without him. But the turrets will stay hot, the fences will stay live, the Klosari will stay penned, and no one will be waiting at the pens when the lights go out. The deal is permanently off.

**Rooms:**

The room hums with a different frequency than the generator — higher, thinner, electrical. Datapads are stacked next to a half-eaten ration pack. A transmission log scrolls on the main display, lines of encrypted text updating every few seconds. Adjacent to Storage to the west and the northeast corridor to the east.
- **Storage** — Weapon crates and supply containers stacked three high against the ancient walls. Imperial-standard packing stencils on some, mercenary tags scratched into others. The room smells of packing grease, old plastoid, and the faint chemical tang of blaster gas. A locked case sits apart from the rest — heavier construction, blast-rated hinges. A portable bacta station sits against the far wall, its amber readout blinking. The cloying sweetness of bacta mixes with preservative chemicals and something organic underneath. A woman in a lab coat works at the bench, her back to the door — absorbed, unhurried. The room connects to the main corridor along the south wall.
- **Central Hall** — The open interior spine of the fortress. One continuous vaulted chamber stretching from the Medical Bay door on the west to the Drainage Sluice stairs on the northeast, and south to the generator housing and the detention corridor. No internal partitions — the builders carved it as a single hall and the mercenaries never bothered to subdivide it. The generator squats in the southern half as a physical obstacle, not a room, thrumming loud enough to hide a whispered conversation two meters away. A notice board near the turbolift entrance carries patrol schedules scratched and rewritten in half a dozen hands. Rubber mats thrown across scuffed basalt flagstones. Work-lights cast hard pools broken by long shadows. This is where two-merc patrol pairs loop every few rounds. If an alarm rings, this is where everyone converges.
- **Generator Room** — A repurposed Clone Wars fusion generator dominates the center of this vaulted chamber — cylindrical, roughly two meters across, thrumming with a bass note you feel in your teeth more than hear. Thick power cables radiate outward from its base like roots, disappearing into conduit channels cut into the stone floor. The air is warm and dry, a sharp contrast to the damp chill everywhere else. Status lights blink in steady green rows along the housing. The hum never wavers, never fluctuates — a heartbeat for the entire building. Through the grated floor plates, you can see drainage channels running with condensation. The room is accessible from the central passage to the north.
- **Turbolift & Stairs** — A Clone Wars-era turbolift car sits in a shaft carved from solid basalt — the car is surplus military, the shaft is ancient. The car rattles when it moves, groaning upward through stone that was never meant to hold it. A narrow staircase winds alongside the shaft, cut directly into the rock — steep, unlit, each step worn smooth by millennia of use before the mercenaries arrived. Both routes lead to the upper level. The turbolift machinery adds its own whine to the generator's hum. The stairwell smells of damp stone and old dust. The junction corridor opens to the north, the east passage to the right, and the detention corridor runs south.
- **Detention Guard Post** — A small enclosed guard room at the south end of the fortress. Two Skarn mercenaries are posted here: a durasteel table, cards spread between them, blasters propped against the wall. A slit viewing-window on the east wall looks directly through the east fence of the third prisoner pen — the guards can see anyone moving toward the fences without standing up. A reinforced door opens north into Central Hall. A second reinforced door in the east wall leads to Varth's Cell. Both can be mag-locked from this room. This post stays sealed and shooting on ORANGE+, no matter what else is happening — their orders are to hold Varth's corridor above all else.
- **Varth's Cell** — A single solo cell — walled off from the prisoner pens, walled off from the general detention area. Durasteel bars welded into an ancient stone frame. The mag-lock glows with a small red indicator light, powered from the central generator. Inside: a cot, a water dispenser, and Admiral Gilder Varth, sitting on the edge of the cot with his legs crossed, watching the doorway with the patient expression of someone who has been expecting company. He doesn't look like a prisoner. He looks like he's waiting for a table at a restaurant. The cell door can be opened from the adjacent Detention Guard Post, or bypassed with a Hard Mechanics check (Security Kit steps it down), or it pops open automatically if the central generator is destroyed.
- **East Wing Pens** — Three holding pens behind shimmering laser fences that cast a dull red glow across the stone floor. The fences hum — a thin, high-pitched whine distinct from the generator's bass throb. Behind the barriers, figures huddle in small groups. Local people — lean, dark-skinned, dressed in woven fiber and leather. Some sit with their arms around their knees. A few stand close to the barriers, watching. Their eyes follow you. The air here is thick with the smell of too many people in too small a space — sweat, fear, old food. A short passage from the generator area leads here. The whine of the fences never stops.
- **Exterior Staging Area** — Cleared ground outside the west wall — open air, jungle visible on every side. The vegetation has been burned back in a rough perimeter, leaving charred stumps and bare red earth. Supply crates are stacked against the building wall in military rows. A squad of stormtroopers stands in formation near the crates — their armor is clean, factory-white, a jarring contrast to the grimy mercenaries inside. They stand at parade rest, watching the jungle treeline with the rigid discipline of soldiers in hostile territory. Boot-prints in the mud lead to a reinforced door in the west wall.
- **Gun Turret — West** — An automated gun emplacement squatting on the western perimeter wall like an armored toad. The barrel tracks in slow, mechanical arcs — scanning, always scanning. Servo motors whir with each sweep. The housing is Clone Wars surplus, pitted and weather-stained but functional. Power cables run from the turret mount down through the wall and into the building interior. The barrel's sweep covers the southern approach and the open ground toward the jungle canopy. When it pivots, the mechanism makes a grinding sound, metal on wet metal.
- **Gun Turret — East** — A second automated turret on the eastern wall, identical to its western twin. This one covers the approach from the drainage sluice side and the jungle slope beyond. The barrel sweeps in patient arcs, tracking nothing, seeing everything. Same Clone Wars housing, same power cables snaking down through the stonework. Rain has stained the turret mount with mineral streaks — orange and grey against the dark basalt.
- **Turbolift Arrival — Level 2** — The turbolift car arrives here with a groan of old hydraulics and settles with a metallic clunk. The shaft is ancient — the builders carved it from solid basalt, and the walls bear the faintest traces of geometric carvings beneath a skin of condensation. The car doors open directly into the upper sanctum. The air is warmer up here, drier, carrying the ozone scent of active electronics from the command area beyond.
- **Command Area — Level 2** — The upper sanctum, repurposed as a command center. A central holo-table projects a slowly rotating terrain map of the surrounding jungle — blue-green contour lines and red sensor pings. Tactical displays line the walls, their glow competing with the ancient carvings they've been hung over — concentric circles radiating from a central point, repeated dozens of times across the stone. Folding field desks are covered in datapads and empty caf cups. Stormtroopers from an advance team have claimed the east corner, standing apart from the mercenary officers with visible tension. The original chamber had an opening in the ceiling — now sealed with a welded durasteel plate. The air hums with competing electronics.
- **Rooftop Landing Pad** — A wide stone platform on the temple roof, open to the sky. The original surface is perfectly level — the ancient builders achieved tolerances that modern engineers would envy. A low parapet, maybe half a meter high, runs along the edges. A Lambda-class shuttle fills most of the pad, wings folded, its angular white hull incongruous against the black basalt. Landing guide-lights and a portable fuel cell have been added by the current occupants. Wind from the jungle canopy below creates unpredictable gusts that tug at clothing and loose equipment. On every side, jungle stretches to the horizon — no structures, no clearings, nothing but an unbroken ocean of green.
