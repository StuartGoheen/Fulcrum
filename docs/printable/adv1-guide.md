# Adventure 1: The Traitor's Gambit
*Act 1 · Adventure 1*

**In-fiction start:** Primeday, 4 Elona, Year 5
*Equivalent to 4 Elona, 7982 C.R.C. (15 BBY). Default day index 1475.*

## Summary
The heroes are drawn into a cantina confrontation on Jakku, discover a stolen Imperial code cylinder, use it to download the coordinates of a secret prison, and break Admiral Varth out of a jungle fortress — all for the promise of a stolen fortune.

## Story Marks (Achievements)
- **Strange Bedfellows** — A stranger crashes your table bleeding. You chose to fight for her instead of walking away.
- **The Devil You Know** — Strike a deal with Switch — a droid who trades in secrets. What you owe him may cost more than credits.
- **Ghost in the Machine** *(hidden)* — Use the Code Cylinder to access the Imperial shuttle's nav computer and learn where it points.
- **Into the Green Hell** *(hidden)* — Survive the jungle of Ajan Kloss — the terrain, the wildlife, and the things that live between the trees.
- **The Warden's Price** *(hidden)* — Mandrake offers a deal: his guidance for your muscle. Honor it or betray it — either way, there's a cost.
- **The Vanishing Place** *(hidden)* — Breach the fortress and reach the man the Empire buried.
- **The Extraction** *(hidden)* — Get Varth out alive. Whatever it takes.
- **The Crucible** — The plan catastrophically fails. Improvise under fire to survive.

**Locations:** jakku, ajan-kloss

## Gear Notes
**Needed:**
- **Breath Mask** — Toxic fungal spores in the Ajan Kloss jungle. Without respiratory protection, characters take environmental penalties on all physical actions during the trek.
- **Syntherope** — Jungle traversal — crossing gaps, climbing, rappelling. Without rope, certain routes become impossible or require much harder Athletics checks. The item everyone forgets to buy.
- **Security Kit** — The Vanishing Place fortress has electronic locks, laser fences, and security panels. Without one, bypassing these is stepped down or impossible.
- **Field Toolkit** — Generator sabotage as part of Mandrake's deal. Without proper tools, Tech actions are stepped down.
- **Starship Tool Kit** — The Banshee is damaged on landing. Without a starship tool kit, all repair rolls are stepped down. Hard permission gate for getting off-world.
- **Medpac** — Multiple combats across both parts with no medical facilities. At least one is non-negotiable.

**Recommended:**
- **Macrobinoculars** — The Reestkii Landing Field read-aloud literally references macrobinoculars for recon. Also useful for jungle scouting.
- **Tox Detector** — Acid pools in the jungle are disguised as standing water. A tox detector identifies them without someone stepping in one.
- **Glow Rod** — Dim cantina, underground bunker, dense jungle canopy — multiple dark environments across both parts.
- **Disguise Kit** — The bluff approach at the Reestkii hangar requires impersonating Imperial crew or authorized personnel.
- **Smoke Grenade** — Covering retreat across open ground during the three-way climax with Shadow Troopers flanking.
- **Stim Pack** — Mid-combat trauma clearance. Valuable in the multi-fight climax sequence.
- **Field Rations** — Extended jungle trek. Fatigue penalties stack after 48 hours without food.
- **Coolth Backpack** — Jakku desert heat in Part 1. Environmental comfort only — no hard gate.
- **Encrypted Comlink** — Coordinating with Maya for extraction timing. Encrypted channel prevents Imperial interception.
- **Datapad (Slicer Config)** — Hacking terminals in the fortress Comms Center to intercept transmissions about Draco's arrival.

## Part 1 — The Jakku Job

### Scene 1.1 — Bad Business at The Burning Deck
*In Media Res*
*Estimated runtime: ~25 min*

#### Read Aloud
> Jakku. A world the galaxy forgot... if it ever noticed. Out here on the Western Reaches, the holonet feeds are weeks old, the Republic is a memory, and the Empire is a rumor that sometimes wears a uniform. You've heard the stories filtering through the spacer lanes: whole systems pacified, Separatist holdouts burned out of their bunkers, the clone army quietly replaced by men who volunteer. The Core worlds call it order. Out here, people just call it the new way things are.
> 
> Reestkii squats on the desert flats like something the sand is trying to swallow. Drifters. Smugglers. Moisture farmers with nothing left to farm. Varga the Hutt runs what passes for an economy: debt, protection, and the quiet understanding that Imperial patrols from Arkanis Sector come through maybe twice a standard year... and they never stay.
> 
> You stepped off a rust-bucket passenger transport twelve hours ago. All of you. However you found each other, you're a crew now, or close enough to one. You eat together. You watch each other's backs. You argue about whose turn it is to pay for fuel. And right now you're waiting together, which might be the hardest part. A fixer named Cade Ryll put the word out through the spacer lanes: crew needed for a short-haul job, good pay, no questions. He named this cantina. He named a time. That was an hour ago.
> 
> The Burning Deck. That's what passes for a watering hole at the edge of civilization. The name's painted over whatever it was called before, which was painted over the name before that. Low ceiling. Failing glow-rods. The air thick with the smell of engine coolant and cheap Corellian whiskey. A Devaronian sits alone at the sabacc table in the far alcove, turning a credit chip across his knuckles. Watching everything. Playing nothing. A pair of Abyssin drifters argue in the corner booth over a rusted motivator, their guttural voices carrying even through the cantina noise. A weathered Togruta woman nurses a drink at the bar, her montrals scarred in a way that says ex-military. She hasn't looked up once, but her hand never strays far from her hip. Behind the bar, a nervous Bith wipes the same glass he's been wiping for twenty minutes, his oversized eyes flicking to the door every time the wind rattles the blast shutters.
> 
> The Burning Deck is quieter than it should be tonight. You noticed it when you docked: a Lambda-class shuttle, sitting on the landing field, flanked by two stormtroopers standing in the heat like they were carved from plastoid. Nobody docks a Lambda on Jakku without a reason. Fewer faces in the stalls on the walk in. The regulars are nursing their drinks a little harder. Talking a little softer. Something has changed the arithmetic, and nobody knows the new numbers yet.
> 
> Your drinks are warm. Your fixer is an hour late. And nothing on Jakku is going the way it was supposed to.
> 
> The blast doors hiss open, and the heat pours in.
> 
> A young human woman. Dark hair, flight jacket, maybe twenty-five. She half-runs, half-stumbles through the entrance. One hand is pressed against her ribs, where her jacket is scorched through. She's bleeding. The Bith bartender freezes mid-wipe. The Devaronian at the sabacc table looks up for the first time all night.
> 
> She makes it four steps before her boot catches a chair leg and she pitches forward, straight into your table. Drinks scatter. Her hands hit the surface and she catches herself, breathing hard, leaving a smear of blood on the table edge. For a second she just stares at you, wild-eyed, like she's trying to decide if you're a wall, or a door.
> 
> "Sorry," she manages between breaths. Then, quieter: "Don't move."
> 
> Before anyone can ask what that means, three figures fill the entrance. A heavy-set Weequay with a scarred knuckle-duster on his right hand, and two Kyuzo in wide-brimmed war helms. Their blasters are holstered. They're not here to kill anyone. They're here to take something back. The Weequay rolls his shoulders, looks at the woman braced against your table, looks at all of you sitting around it, and makes a calculation.
> 
> "The girl took something that belongs to Varga the Hutt," he says. "This doesn't have to involve anyone else."

#### GM Notes
TWO-BEAT STRUCTURE: This scene is split into two read-aloud blocks. Read Part 1 (the cantina, the crew waiting, the patrons, the atmosphere) and then PAUSE. Ask 'What do you do while you wait?' Let the players explore the room, talk to each other, interact with patrons, investigate the Imperial shuttle — whatever they want. This is their first moment in the world. Don't rush it. When the energy is right — or when they need a push — read Part 2 (Maya's entrance).

  CADE RYLL — THE TRUTH (GM ONLY, NEVER REVEAL AT THE TABLE): "Cade Ryll" is a cover identity. The man behind the name is a deep-cover Hidden Path operative — a survivor of the Jedi Purge who has spent the last four years building a small underground railroad for Force-sensitive children and Jedi remnants. Among Force-sensitives he is unusually good at reading Shatterpoints — the moments where the smallest push tips an enormous amount of weight — and weeks ago he saw the line that runs from this cantina, through Maya, through a stolen Imperial code cylinder, all the way out to ancient Jedi navigational holocrons he has been hunting for two years. The crew at this table is the only domino he sets in the entire campaign. Everything that follows is supposed to fall on its own. SURFACE PLAY GUIDANCE IS UNCHANGED: he never shows, no NPC arrives in his place, and the meet pivots to Maya. WHY HE IS NOT HERE: two reasons. First, the Lambda on the landing field is two days into a tightening Imperial sweep and his face is on a list — being in this room would get him killed and this crew arrested. Second and more important, his presence at the table would destroy the moral pressure the Shatterpoint needs to land on the heroes themselves; if a Jedi is sitting between them and Maya, they fight for the stranger because the man with the lightsaber said to. He needs them to choose. WHY HE IS AN HOUR LATE: he is not late by accident. He scheduled the meet to be exactly empty by the time Maya arrived, calculated from her last known movement pattern. She took roughly ninety minutes longer than he expected — the only beat he did not see coming. PLANT THE TELL (this matters later): the Devaronian sitting alone in the west alcove turning a credit chip across his knuckles is the watcher's first quiet cameo — the "Siv Keel" identity described below in THE PATRONS is his cover for this scene, not a separate NPC. Make the chip-on-knuckles gesture noticeable in passing — a beat of description, nothing more. He never speaks to the crew, leaves before the fight, and is gone by the time anyone thinks to look for him. From here on, every adventure or two, an unnamed stranger somewhere in the scene will turn a credit chip across their knuckles. Different cover, same hand. The reveal lands long after Adventure 10. If the players ask around for "Cade Ryll," nobody at The Burning Deck knows the name — keep it that way. The full lore for the watcher and the Hidden Path lives in the campaign bible (Major Characters → The Watcher; Faction Tracker → The Hidden Path). Do not narrate any of this. Do not hint at his identity, his Force-sensitivity, or his faction. Let the breadcrumb sit.

  THE PATRONS: The cantina regulars are set dressing with depth if the players engage. The Devaronian (sabacc alcove) is a small-time gambler named Siv Keel — he knows the cantina well and can tell the PCs that the Burning Deck has been tense since the Lambda landed two days ago (Day 2, Month 1, Year 5 — GM clarity only). The Abyssin drifters are arguing over a water claim in the badlands — they're loud but harmless and can point the PCs toward the deep desert if asked. The Togruta at the bar is Nela Bren — an ex-Republic military scout who deserted after Order 66. She won't volunteer much but responds to respect; she can confirm Imperial patrols have increased in the sector. The Bith bartender, Dodd Mull, is terrified — he owes Varga money and is afraid any trouble in his establishment will bring the Hutt's people down on him. If weapons come out, the patrons scatter — most for the front entrance, a few ducking down the back hallway behind the bar.

  MAYA'S BACKSTORY (GM ONLY): Maya intercepted an Imperial Code Cylinder before Varga's operators could get their hands on it. She knew it was valuable — encrypted Imperial data, potentially worth a fortune to the right buyer. She thought she could deal with Varga: cut him in, negotiate a split, walk away clean. But Varga doesn't negotiate over things he already considers his. The cylinder was moving through his supply chain. In his eyes, she stole from him. He sent Krev's crew to recover it. They caught up to her outside the cantina — Krev put a warning shot into her ribs to slow her down. She ran anyway. The foot chase ended at The Burning Deck's blast doors.

  MAYA'S ENTRANCE: This is NOT an assassination scene. The enforcers are a retrieval crew for Varga's operation — they're here to recover the cylinder, not kill Maya. They have NOT drawn weapons inside the cantina. Maya's collision with the heroes' table looks like an accident — she tripped on a chair leg while running wounded. But the GM should know: she was already calculating. She clocked the armed crew at the table on her way in and angled toward them. The trip was real — she's hurt and off-balance — but the direction she ran was deliberate. The players should NOT know this yet. The Insight check (sc-s1-read-maya) is the gate for that information. To the players, she's just a wounded stranger who crashed into their table at the worst possible time.

  MAYA'S DIALOGUE: 'Sorry' and 'Don't move' are deliberately ambiguous. She's not explicitly asking them to play along or be her shield. She's just a hurt person trying to survive the next thirty seconds. The enforcers see her at the crew's table and make the assumption themselves — 'This doesn't have to involve anyone else' implies THEY think there might be a connection. The heroes can choose to confirm or deny that assumption.

  NARRATIVE PACING: This scene runs on narrative pacing. Do NOT call for dice unless failure forks the story. Maya crashing in — narrative. Krev's ultimatum — narrative. The bartender's nervousness — narrative. Only roll when a PC takes an action where failure has real consequences (escalation, loss of initiative, or missed intelligence).

  THE IMPERIAL SHUTTLE: Foreshadowing only. The Lambda on the landing field is not part of this scene — it seeds future tension. If players investigate during the pause, the stormtroopers say nothing. Locals don't know why it's here. Dodd the bartender nervously changes the subject. This pays off later.

  MAYA'S WOUND: Real. A blaster graze to her side — Krev's warning shot outside the cantina to slow her down during the foot chase. Non-lethal but painful and bleeding. She is desperate — genuinely — but she's also smarter than she looks.

  RESOLUTION PATHS:
  • QUICK-DRAW INTIMIDATE: A Gunslinger draws fast — blaster out before the enforcers can react. This IS the Intimidate check (Moderate), not a separate action. The draw is the statement. The blaster is leverage, not escalation. Success: Krev folds ('Not worth it today'). Failure: Krev calls the bluff ('You won't shoot over some girl you don't know') — now the PC must decide: pull the trigger (firefight) or holster (Krev smells weakness, uses Lean In).
  • SOCIAL: Charm, Deception, or Persuasion (Moderate) can convince the enforcers Maya isn't worth the trouble, redirect them, or negotiate a delay.
  • BRAWL: If anyone throws a punch, the Kyuzo respond in kind. Bar brawl — fists, headbutts, bottles. Stun damage. Blasters stay holstered.
  • FIREFIGHT: Only triggers if (a) a brawl is already happening AND someone draws a blaster, OR (b) the social check fails AND a PC with blaster already drawn refuses to hand Maya over. A drawn blaster alone is not an escalation — it's a negotiating tool. This is Star Wars.

  IF COMBAT TRIGGERS — [Join Battle] Reference:
  • Control Step-Down: Highest enemy Tier = 1 → step down Control Die 1 time
  • Failed Control (1-3): PC is surprised — [Disoriented] + [Exposed] until end of first turn
  • Mastery (8+): PC designates one enemy as surprised — [Disoriented] + [Exposed] on that NPC
  • Power Die result = initiative slot (higher acts first). NPCs act at the static Initiative shown on their stat block
  • NPC static initiative: Krev = 5, Kyuzo = 5

  Scene Tactics (NPC-agnostic — survives any NPC swap):
  • Hostiles enter through the front entrance. Expect a heavy/anchor type to push the PCs' table and lock down the social-pressure space; lighter/faster bodies will try to flank along the booth row and the alcove.
  • The center floor is the kill zone — no cover, clear sightlines. The booth row and the alcove offer light cover. The bar counter is hard cover with improvised weapons (bottles, glasses) within reach.
  • The back hallway behind the bar is the only secondary route out. The store room is a trap — full cover, no exit.
  • Door controls at the front entrance can be slammed to cut off reinforcements OR to trap the PCs inside (GM's call based on stakes).
  • Failing glow-rods make light unreliable — GMs can rule darkness/concealment tactical effects as the brawl moves.
  • Brawl rules: stun damage, improvised weapons, blasters stay holstered. If a blaster comes out, the scene shifts to lethal.
  • Bystanders scatter when weapons appear — most break for the front entrance, a few duck down the back hallway.

  KEY LORE: 15 BBY. Imperial presence on Jakku is thin — Arkanis Sector patrols, no garrison. The Lambda shuttle today is unusual and unsettling. Varga the Hutt runs the real economy here: smuggling, debt, protection. A cantina fight draws local attention (Varga's people, scavenger gangs) but NOT Imperial attention — the troopers at the landing field don't care about Hutt business.

#### Pacing
- **Opening:** Part 1 establishes Jakku, the crew's identity, the missing fixer, and the subdued cantina. Players get time to explore the room and its patrons. The pause gives them ownership of the space before the plot arrives.
- **Rising action:** Part 2 — Maya crashes in wounded. Thugs fill the doorway. Krev delivers his ultimatum. Kyuzo silently flank. The crew must decide whether this stranger's problem is their problem.
- **Climax:** The decision — fast-draw intimidate, social play, or let it go to fists. One roll that matters.
- **Resolution:** The fight is done. The cantina is wrecked. The thugs are gone — one way or another. Maya is still here, bleeding at the table, watching you with an expression that is not quite gratitude.

#### Key Checks
**ASSESS** — investigation (presence) vs Maya [resist]
- *Context:* Reading Maya — is that collision really an accident? Only roll if a PC specifically says they're trying to read her. Failure matters: it hides that she angled toward their table on purpose, which colors trust going forward.
- *Pacing:* Roll only if asked. Do not prompt for this check.
- **Failure:** Maya reads as a desperate woman who tripped at the worst possible moment. Pure bad luck. No indication she chose their table — which she did.
