I watched the *Banshee* settle onto Takodana, the rain already streaking the hull. At Blackwind Point, Kessra and The Vipers met them with the landing tax. I noted Reyla Sun’s quick decision to pay the bribe. It bought them a smooth departure from the dock, no wasted time or collateral damage, which was efficient. But it came at a cost, as Reyla herself later marked, a feeling of compromise. I could see the practicality of that choice, the avoidance of unnecessary conflict, but I also considered what it would have meant to stand firm, to refuse the shakedown. That path would have carried more risk, certainly, but perhaps a different kind of integrity, a refusal to bend. Reyla’s words suggested a tension within their own ranks on that point, a discomfort with the easy way out even when it was the logical one.

Their search for Warrick Raden led them through the encampment, a slow process I am still observing. Nola Shar and Gorr the Barkeep offered little, and the interactions with Mekka, Drelk, and Greez Thakka did not seem to yield much immediate progress. They eventually located Raden at the Filtration Plant. There, I marked another choice: to cut him loose. This decision prompted a significant argument among the crew, lasting an hour, which I found interesting. On one hand, it freed them from an immediate entanglement, allowing them to focus on their primary objective without the burden of a new dependant or complication. It streamlined their operation. On the other hand, it severed a potential connection, perhaps a source of future information or an ally. It showed a willingness to discard, which can be both a strength and a vulnerability, depending on the situation. The internal debate suggested not everyone agreed on the value of that kind of efficiency.

Now, the situation around Raden has escalated. I observed the arrival of Trandoshan Mercenaries, a hit squad intent on eliminating him. Raden’s Sentry Droid was destroyed quickly. The combat is still unfolding, and I am watching to see how Reyla, Old Pike, and Mott handle the sudden violence. Old Pike’s comment about hearing a voice in the corridor, sleeping badly, suggested a growing unease even before this. It seemed a small thing, but sometimes the small things are the ones that matter. I am curious to see if this current engagement will solidify their approach to these kinds of entanglements, or if it will simply add more friction to their already strained dynamic. I am watching to see what lessons they draw from this.
