I watched the pirate fleet gather. Oga Garra's armada, a surprising array, moved toward Malpaz. The Leviathan waited. Its arms opened, crescent shapes against the stars. The sky over the planet began to distort, a subtle ripple at first, then larger movements as asteroids shifted orbit. They were flying into the jaws, just as I expected.

I marked their first choice on the approach: paying the bribe. Reyla Sun later wrote that she did not love what they became to make it cheap. She was right, in a way. It was a compromise, a small surrender of principle for convenience. It bought them clear passage without a fight, preserving their strength and time for the greater threat. But it also revealed a willingness to bend, to deal with petty corruption, which could be exploited later. It showed a pragmatism in Reyla, a captain's calculation of resources, but also a lingering unease about the cost to their own perceived integrity.

Later, I noted the decision to "cut them loose." It caused an argument among the crew, I heard. This choice, too, had its angles. It served the immediate mission, shedding a potential burden, keeping their focus tight on the objective. A hard, efficient call in a desperate situation. But it also meant leaving others behind, abandoning a connection. It demonstrated a cold resolve for some, perhaps a necessary one, but for others, it was a heavy weight. That internal friction, the hour-long argument, revealed the different tolerances for such decisions within the group.

Then the engagement began. I watched Soren Vex's *Requital* intercept the missile meant for the *Banshee*. His ship took the brunt of it, hull breached, engines dead. His final comms, cut short. Maya watched it happen, fixed on the screen, but the battle did not pause for grief. The confrontation with Varth was inevitable, a reckoning for the trust he had so carefully cultivated and then shattered. And with Draco, the deeper cut. I saw them find Denia, broken but alive, a ghost from their past. Varth's cold calculus in leaving her to their custody, revealed in that moment. It was a quiet punch, seeing the full extent of that manipulation laid bare. Old Pike, in his notes, mentioned hearing a voice in the corridor, sleeping badly. He dismissed it as nothing. I did not.

I am still turning over the cost of these choices, the balance between survival and what they are willing to lose. I will watch to see if the weight of these decisions changes their next moves, or if they simply carry it forward.
