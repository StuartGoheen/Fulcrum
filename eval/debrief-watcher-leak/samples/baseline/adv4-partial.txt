I watched them make planetfall on the Forest Moon, a place far greener than I might have expected, given the journey. Admiral Varth stood at the center of their briefing, as always, while Master Denia remained somewhat apart, her counsel given but not always taken. Maya, I noted, kept the Banshee ready, her focus practical. Their first major decision came at the dock: to pay the bribe. It bought them smooth passage, no immediate complications, and preserved their resources, a pragmatic choice for a crew on a schedule. But I also marked Vex Marrin's later comment, the feeling that something was lost in that ease, a compromise that sat uncomfortably with at least one of them. It was efficient, yes, but it revealed a willingness to bend to the system rather than challenge it, a clear priority for expediency over other considerations.

Their path into the forest began with a quick, violent encounter with a boar-wolf, a reminder of the moon's wildness. Then came the choice to cut loose whatever target they had acquired. I observed the immediate friction this caused within the crew, an argument that stretched for a full hour. For some, I imagine it felt like a necessary detachment, shedding a potential liability to maintain focus on the primary objective. For others, it likely felt like an unresolved loose end, a decision that left questions hanging or perhaps created a future problem. It highlighted the different tolerances for risk and responsibility that exist among them, a constant undercurrent. Hara Ko, I marked, seemed particularly watchful, noting a voice in the corridor that she did not place, a small detail that suggests a heightened awareness of their environment, or perhaps of something more.

Now, I am watching them approach the Ewoks. The Canopy Ambush began, and the situation with the War Party is still unfolding. The choice between parley or pyre, as they frame it, will reveal another layer of their approach to the unknown. Will they seek common ground, or will they assert their will? The outcome is not yet certain, but the stakes are clear: access to the Dead Forest and, beyond that, Revan's Spire. That petrified zone, I know, is strong with a tangible, ancient weight, a pressure that will test them in ways a bribe or a quick fight cannot.

I am curious to see how they navigate the Dead Forest, and what the ancient Spire itself will demand of them. The charts they seek are only part of the puzzle, and I am still turning over what they will reveal about themselves when faced with the echoes of the past.
