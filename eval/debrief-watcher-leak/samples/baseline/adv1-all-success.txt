I watched Maya crash into their orbit at The Burning Deck, a predictable entry for someone running. The Kyuzo skirmishers were standard muscle, easily distracted. I marked Kira, Tarn, and Sable's initial reactions: a mix of caution and opportunism. Their first significant choice came quickly, at the dock. They chose to pay Krev Tosk's bribe. It bought them smooth passage, avoiding immediate violence and the attention that would have followed. Kira noted it was "cheaper than a fight," and indeed it was, in credits and immediate risk. But it also cost them, forcing them to submit to a petty shakedown. The alternative, a stand, would have signaled a different kind of crew, but would have undoubtedly complicated their departure from Jakku. They chose discretion over confrontation, a telling first move.

Maya's offer was straightforward enough: the code cylinder, Varth, a promise of wealth. I observed their pursuit of Switch, the processor, and the subsequent entanglement with Ganga Lor. The confrontation was messy, as these things often are. When it came to Switch himself, I marked their second choice: to take him alive. This decision bought them potential leverage, or at least kept certain doors open, but it clearly came at a cost. The crew "argued for an hour after," indicating a clear division in their ranks. To simply eliminate the threat would have been more efficient, less complicated, but they chose a path that required more effort, more negotiation, both internally and externally. It speaks to a certain internal friction, a reluctance to fully commit to the most expedient solution.

I noted Kira's reflection, that she did not "love what we became to make it cheap" after the bribe. This suggests an internal accounting, a awareness of the compromises being made, even when practical. Tarn's restless night, hearing a "voice in the corridor that I did not place," is a small detail, but I log it. Could be nothing. Could be the weight of the day, or something else entirely. They are making choices that have consequences beyond the immediate transaction.

I am still turning over the implications of these choices, particularly the internal disagreements they reveal. I am watching to see how quickly they are willing to set aside their own discomforts for the sake of the larger objective, and what that larger objective will ultimately demand of them.
