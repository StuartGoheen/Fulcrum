I watched them track Varth to Eriadu. The trail was clear enough, if one knew where to look. Their reconnaissance of the Citadel was thorough, or thorough enough for their immediate needs. I marked their first public move on the docks, choosing to pay the bribe. It bought them smooth passage, no immediate complications, which was their clear objective at that moment. But it also cost them a measure of self, as Vex Marrin later noted, seeing themselves changed by the transaction. The alternative, a direct confrontation, would have asserted something else, perhaps a different kind of control, but at the cost of immediate attention and potential delays. A pragmatic choice, then, but one that left a mark on at least one of them.

I watched them infiltrate the Citadel. From my vantage, I had a clear view as Varth stood before Tarkin. I saw the exchange, the formal transfer of command for the Leviathan. It was a clean transaction, devoid of sentiment, but loaded with consequence. I marked the moment the crew understood what they were witnessing. All their efforts, all the risks they had taken, had served this larger, hidden agenda. The promise of a payday, the Vel Shara score, revealed itself as a carefully constructed deception, a leash. It bought Varth the power he sought, but it cost the crew their fundamental trust, transforming their understanding of their own past actions.

The immediate aftermath was chaotic. I watched them engage the Death Troopers, then Draco's Apprentice. The fight was sharp, precise. I noted their choice regarding the target they had. They chose to cut them loose, a move that led to an hour of internal argument, as I later gathered. It bought them speed, an unencumbered exit, focusing resources on their primary objective: slicing the Primary Data Center for Varth's orders. But it cost them internal cohesion, creating a rift, a clear division in their priorities. The opposite choice might have slowed them, added risk, but perhaps solidified their collective purpose or gained them something unexpected. I also made a note of Hara Ko’s unease later, the voice she reported hearing in the corridor. She dismissed it as nothing, but I marked it regardless.

I watched them slice the data center, confirming the full scope of what Varth had done. The transit orders, the destination — all laid bare. Their escape through the acid rain of Eriadu’s industrial landscape was swift, almost desperate. The navigation data they had worked so hard to assemble was now firmly in Imperial hands, and the Leviathan, under Varth's command, was now a tangible threat. Their mission, once defined by profit, had irrevocably shifted. It was no longer about what they could gain, but what they had inadvertently helped to unleash.

I am still turning over the implications of that data, the true scope of what Varth now commands. I will be watching closely to see how they navigate this new reality, and what choices they make when the cost is no longer merely financial.
