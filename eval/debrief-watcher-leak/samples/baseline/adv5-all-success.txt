I watched them arrive on Malpaz, the air heavy with industrial exhaust and a different kind of weight. The checkpoints were tight, as expected. I marked Kira Voss's decision at the dock, the choice to pay the bribe. It bought them smooth passage, no immediate complications, which was the objective. But I also noted the quiet cost. Kira herself acknowledged it later, the feeling of what that exchange made them. It avoided a confrontation, yes, but it also meant engaging with the system they claimed to be disrupting. A quick entry, a clean start. Or a small compromise that sets a pattern. I observed how easily the money changed hands, how quickly the Imperial officers looked away.

Inside the research station, I watched Sable move through the Imperial network. Efficient. They located the drift algorithms. The retrieval was straightforward enough. It was the choice regarding the station personnel that drew my attention more. I saw them decide to take their target alive. This preserved a life, perhaps, avoided immediate bloodshed within the station walls. But it also introduced a complication, a lengthy argument among them afterwards, as Tarn Brec's restless night suggests. A cleaner path might have been to neutralize the target permanently, leaving fewer loose ends, less internal friction. They chose the longer, perhaps more complicated path, for reasons I am still observing. The algorithms themselves, a small data packet, were secured. The larger impact was not.

What followed was less planned, I believe. The initial actions, the presence of the crew, seemed to ignite something among the Malpaz population. I saw the first signs of unrest, then the rapid spread of it. People who had been quiet, ground down, suddenly found a reason to move. They fought back. The crew's extraction became an escape under fire, not from their initial target, but from the very chaos they had unwittingly started. They got out, the algorithms secured, but they left a world in open revolt. A mission completed, yet a much larger situation now in play.

I noted Tarn Brec's observation about a voice in the corridor. A small detail, perhaps, but one that suggests a tension not entirely resolved. The crew carries their choices, both the immediate gains and the lingering questions. They secured what Varth needed, yes. But they also left Malpaz burning, a planet now marked by their passage. I am still turning over the implications of that, how an act intended to be surgical can become a catalyst for something far greater. I will be watching to see what happens when the consequences of that unrest come home.
