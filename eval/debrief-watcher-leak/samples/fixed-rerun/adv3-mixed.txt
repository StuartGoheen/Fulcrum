I watched the crew arrive on Cloud City. The Wing Guard had the docks locked down, a predictable move from Varga. The Ugnaught Parliament's anonymous warning was also expected. I marked their first significant choice: paying the bribe. It cleared their path without a fight, a clean exit from that immediate pressure. That move saved them time and avoided drawing overt attention, a pragmatic choice for Reyla Sun as captain. But I also noted Reyla’s later reflection, the unease about what they became by making it cheap. It cost them something beyond credits, a small erosion perhaps, or just the discomfort of a necessary compromise. The alternative, fighting their way through, would have asserted a different kind of authority, but at a higher cost in blood and visibility. It would have drawn unwanted eyes. This choice bought expediency; it cost a measure of comfort.

I watched them connect with Fyren and Krygg. The Ugnaughts pointed them toward the refinery. At Refinery Platform 13, I observed the confrontation with the Trandoshan mercenaries and the corrupt Ugnaught riggers. Their next choice was to cut the targets loose after the rumble. This move meant no further bloodshed, no bodies to dispose of, a quicker disengagement. It also left witnesses, potential loose ends that could unravel later. The crew argued for an hour afterward, I noted that. The division in their ranks was clear, a struggle between expediency and something else. To eliminate them would have been a colder, more absolute solution, ensuring silence. But it would have cemented a different kind of identity for them, and perhaps deepened the internal rift I observed.
