I watched them follow the faint trail to Eriadu, a quiet pursuit through the outer systems. The atmosphere here was thick with industry, the air itself a caustic reminder of what power consolidates. I marked their careful reconnaissance of Tarkin's Citadel, a grey, imposing structure that seemed to drink the acid rain. They moved with a practiced caution, a rhythm I have observed many times before.

At the dock, I noted their initial decision: they chose to pay the bribe. It was a clean transaction, clearing their path without incident. This bought them swift passage and avoided any immediate conflict, a pragmatic move. But I also marked the cost of it, the quiet discomfort that settled over Vex Marrin. I heard him later, turning it over, speaking of "what we became." It was a small compromise, perhaps, but it revealed a hairline fracture in their resolve, a line they crossed that some part of them still resisted. Another path would have invited a fight, certainly, but it would have kept them clear of that particular stain. The choice showed their desire for efficiency, but also the internal price some of them pay for it.

Later, I observed them make another call, choosing to "cut them loose." It was a decision regarding someone or something connected to the trail they were following, a thread they opted not to pull further. This choice saved them immediate entanglement, perhaps, or kept their focus narrow on Varth. But the crew argued for an hour afterward, the disagreement a clear sign of the unaddressed consequences, the potential information or future complications they might have sacrificed. I saw the tension it created, the different priorities at play. Soon after, from my vantage, I watched Varth step onto the Glass Bridge. I watched him meet Moff Tarkin. I saw the formal transfer, the command of the Leviathan granted. It was a clear, cold moment. I saw the pieces click into place for them, the true nature of their employer, the real prize he had sought all along. The navigation data they had delivered, the "payday" that had lured them here — it was all a means to this end.

The confrontation came quickly then. I watched them fight their way through Death Troopers, through Draco's Apprentice, a frantic scramble for answers. I observed them at the Primary Data Center, working to slice Varth's orders, to confirm the scope of the deception. The data confirmed it all, laying bare how they had built the road to this moment, unknowingly serving a far larger design. Their escape through the acid rain was desperate. Vex's earlier words about "what we became" took on a heavier meaning in the downpour. Hara Ko, too, seemed unsettled, speaking of voices in the corridor, a general unease. The shift in their purpose was palpable, from pursuit of payment to something else entirely.

I am still turning over what this new understanding will do to them. I will watch to see if this betrayal hardens their resolve or fractures it further. The Leviathan is now in play, and their path has irrevocably changed.
