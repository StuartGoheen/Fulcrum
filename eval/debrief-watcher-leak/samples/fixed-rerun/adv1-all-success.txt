I watched the human, Maya, stumble into The Burning Deck, her state clearly indicating a chase. The crew, Kira Voss, Tarn Brec, and Sable, found themselves drawn into her situation quickly, as Krev Tosk and his Kyuzo skirmishers followed. It was a swift escalation. When the opportunity arose to clear the dock, I marked their choice to pay the bribe. It bought them immediate passage, avoiding a direct confrontation with Tosk and his crew, which likely saved them time and resources in that moment. However, it also meant accepting the terms of someone like Tosk, a transaction Kira Voss later noted she did not "love," despite the immediate practical benefit. It was a pragmatic move, one that valued expediency over principle, or perhaps simply chose a lesser cost for a quicker exit.

Maya laid out her offer, the code cylinder the key. Their next move led them to Switch. I observed them make the call to take the target alive. This choice meant a longer, more complicated engagement than a swift elimination might have offered. It bought them the potential for information, or perhaps simply avoided unnecessary bloodshed, showing a certain restraint in a situation where lethal force would have been easier. But it cost them dearly in time and internal cohesion; the argument that followed, as I noted, lasted for a full hour. It revealed a split in their approach, some valuing the 'alive' condition, others likely seeing it as an inefficient risk. It was a decision that forced them to confront not just their target, but each other.

The situation with Switch quickly devolved into combat, with Ganga Lor and his own thugs entering the fray. I watched the crew navigate the chaos. They handled the overlapping threats, the sudden shift in alliances or animosities on the ground. It was a messy engagement, one that underscored the unpredictable nature of these backwater operations. Tarn Brec later mentioned hearing a voice he couldn't place in the corridor after the fight, a small detail I noted. Some things, even in the quiet moments, seem to register.

I am still turning over the implications of their choices, particularly the internal friction that arose from the decision to take Switch alive. I will watch to see if this pattern of internal debate over the cost of their methods persists, and how it shapes their future actions. The fortune Varth promised is a powerful motivator, but I am curious about what other currencies they will find themselves trading in.
