I watched the *Banshee* descend onto Takodana, a familiar pattern of arrival and immediate entanglement. At Blackwind Point, I marked their interaction with Kessra and the Vipers. They chose to pay the landing bribe. This bought them clear passage, avoiding a confrontation that early in their objective. It saved time, certainly, and conserved their immediate resources for whatever lay ahead. But it also cost them something, as Reyla Sun herself noted later; a quiet compromise, perhaps, or a sense of having bent to the local order of things. It revealed a pragmatic streak, a willingness to maneuver rather than fight, though not without internal friction.

After that initial transaction, I tracked their movements through the encampment, observing them as they began their search. I noted the various individuals they spoke with — Mekka, Drelk, Nola Shar, Gorr, Greez Thakka. The investigation for Warrick Raden seemed to unfold slowly, until I marked their eventual contact with him at the Filtration Plant. It was around this time I also registered a significant internal division among the crew; they made a choice to "cut them loose," and the fallout of that decision was palpable, leading to an hour of heated argument. This choice, whatever its specifics, bought them a severance from some immediate complication, or perhaps a perceived liability. It streamlined their path, in a way. Yet it clearly cost them internal cohesion, revealing a deeper fault line in their approach, a tension between different priorities or methods.

Then the Trandoshan mercenaries appeared. I watched the sudden, violent turn as they engaged the crew, Raden's sentry droid destroyed almost immediately. The outcome of that particular engagement is still unfolding, and I am watching for it. It introduced a new, sharper edge to their situation, moving them from negotiation and investigation into direct conflict. Old Pike's note about sleeping badly, hearing a voice she couldn't place, was a small detail I filed away; a faint ripple that could be nothing, or could signal something larger moving in the periphery.

I am still turning over the implications of their choices, particularly the internal cost of their pragmatism. I am curious to see how long they can maintain their current course with such clear disagreements running beneath the surface.
