I watched them make planetfall on Malpaz, a world worn down, as expected. The first choice I marked was at the dock, when they opted to pay the bribe. It was a pragmatic move, certainly. It bought them clear passage without drawing immediate Imperial attention, keeping their timeline tight and their presence quiet. But I noted Kira Voss’s thoughts on it later; she seemed to weigh the cost beyond the credits, a quiet unease about what such an easy transaction might imply about them. It was efficient for the immediate objective, but I sensed it settled poorly with at least one among them.

Inside the research station, I observed them retrieving the drift algorithms, the last piece Varth needed. Their next significant choice was what to do with the station personnel. They chose to take them alive. This led to a lengthy argument among the crew, an hour of back-and-forth that could have been spent moving faster. It complicated their extraction, adding a layer of risk and logistical challenge. Yet, it also prevented an escalation of violence, preserving lives and perhaps keeping options open for later, though I could not discern any immediate strategic benefit. It seems some among them still hold to a certain line, even when under pressure, even when it costs time and creates friction within their own ranks.

Their extraction from Malpaz was less clean than their arrival. The algorithms secured, they departed under fire. Their actions, the infiltration, the disruption, had an unintended consequence. I watched the local populace begin to move, an uprising spreading across the world they were leaving behind. What started as a discreet retrieval job became something much larger, a spark for a full-scale insurrection. Tarn Brec's note about hearing a voice in the corridor, dismissed by him as 'probably nothing,' was also something I marked. Sometimes, 'nothing' bears watching.

I am still turning over the implications of that insurrection. They acquired what they came for, but at what larger cost for Malpaz itself? I will watch to see if this pattern of unintended consequences, of smaller actions triggering larger events, continues to follow them, and what it means for the worlds they touch.
