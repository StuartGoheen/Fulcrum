I watched the pirate fleet assemble, a motley collection under Oga Garra, preparing to throw themselves against what Varth had unleashed on Malpaz. A strange sight, that many disparate interests aligned for a common, desperate cause. The scale of it was immense, the desperation palpable, even from my distance. They moved into the Malpaz Corridor, into the jaws of the gravity well array, and I am still watching that struggle unfold.

Earlier, I had marked their choice at the dock. Reyla Sun chose to pay the bribe. It bought them smooth passage, no immediate conflict, no wasted time or resources on a minor skirmish. A practical decision, in some ways. But it cost them something, too, beyond the credits. I registered Reyla's own words later; she did not like what they became, making it cheap. It was a choice that prioritized expediency, perhaps, over a different kind of conviction, one that might have seen them refuse to deal. I considered what the opposite choice would have revealed about them.

I also noted their decision to cut loose with the target. It gave them freedom, untethered them from another potential complication, allowed them to focus on the immediate objective. But it left others behind, a different kind of cost. I observed the crew argue for an hour after that call, the friction of it evident even from a distance. It showed a divide among them, some prioritizing the mission, others perhaps wrestling with what they left in their wake. Later, I marked Old Pike’s restless shift. He mentioned hearing a voice he did not place in the corridor, dismissed it as nothing. I made a note of it.

In the unfolding chaos of the Malpaz Corridor, I watched Soren Vex make his final move. He diverted the missile, saving the *Banshee*. His ship, the *Requital*, took the hit instead. Hull breached, engines dead, life support failing. I heard the comms cut, watched Maya register it. The battle did not pause for grief. And then I saw the discovery of Denia, alive, in Draco’s custody. Broken, interrogated for years, but surviving. A profound cost, that one, given Varth’s previous counsel to abandon her, now revealed as part of a deeper betrayal. He had given her to the Inquisition. The layers of it are still settling.

I am still turning over the implications of these revelations, especially for Maya and the others. I will be watching closely to see how they navigate the wreckage Varth left behind, and what new choices they make, now that the full truth of his manipulations has been exposed.
