I watched the situation unfold in The Burning Deck. Maya arrived, bleeding, drawing the attention of Krev Tosk and his Kyuzo enforcers. The crew, facing immediate trouble, made a choice. They paid the bribe. It cost them credits, a small sum, but it bought them clear passage and avoided a direct confrontation that would have drawn more attention to Maya, and to them. Kira Voss later noted she did not love what they became to make it cheap, which suggests a lingering discomfort with the pragmatism. Had they chosen violence, they might have saved the money and asserted control, but they would have also risked injury, attracted Imperial notice, and potentially delayed whatever Maya was running from. It was a choice for expediency over principle, or perhaps, for survival over pride. I marked it.

After Maya made her offer – the code cylinder, the promise of Varth, the fortune – they pursued the lead to Switch. There, they faced another decision: whether to take their targets alive. They chose to. This choice bought them potential information, perhaps leverage down the line, and avoided unnecessary killing. It also led to an hour of argument among them, a cost in internal cohesion and time. This suggests a split in their priorities, some valuing information or a moral line, others perhaps favoring a cleaner, quicker solution. Had they chosen to eliminate Switch and his thugs, it would have been simpler, no arguments, no loose ends, but they would have lost any intel those individuals might have possessed. It reveals a complex dynamic, where expediency sometimes clashes with other considerations.

I observed their movements through the Sinkhole, the confrontation with Switch, and later, the combat with Ganga Lor's thugs. They moved with a certain cohesion when pressed, despite their earlier disagreements. Tarn Brec mentioned hearing a voice in the corridor, which I noted. It was probably nothing. They handled the situation with a blend of caution and effectiveness, a pattern I have observed before in similar crews. They are learning to move together, a necessary adaptation given the company they now keep and the stakes involved.

I am still turning over what this particular crew values most. The initial choices suggest a strong leaning towards self-preservation and profit, but the arguments indicate deeper currents beneath the surface. I will watch to see if those currents pull them in different directions, or if they find a way to reconcile them.
