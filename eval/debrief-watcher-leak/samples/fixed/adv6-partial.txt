I watched them arrive on Eriadu, following Varth's trail through the industrial haze. Their entry at the dock was smooth enough, marked by the choice to pay the bribe. It bought them passage without incident, a quick clearing of the way. I noted Vex Marrin's later reflection on that choice, the quiet unease about what it cost them, even if it saved them a fight. Hara Ko, too, seemed to carry a shadow from it, a restless night. It was a practical decision, certainly, valuing expediency over a stand, but it left a small, lingering mark on them, a testament to the quiet toll of such calculations. Someone else might have chosen a different path, accepting immediate conflict for a different kind of clarity. 

I watched them infiltrate the Citadel, a stark, formidable structure. From my vantage, I observed the moment the truth solidified for them: Varth, standing with Tarkin himself, receiving command of the Leviathan. The objective, which had been simple enough – tracking a target, securing a payday – shifted in that instant. I marked the clear change in Varth's role, from their calculating benefactor to the architect of something far larger, far more dangerous. The navigation data, once their prize, was now clearly a key piece in a puzzle they had unknowingly helped assemble. 

I observed their subsequent actions, the combat against the Death Troopers and Draco's Apprentice, a swift and brutal confirmation of the stakes. Their decision to slice the Primary Data Center was a critical one. It cost them time, drew more attention, and put them in direct danger, but it bought them the definitive proof, Varth's transit orders, the full scope of his betrayal. It was a clear move to understand the depth of what they had stumbled into, rather than simply escaping. I also marked the internal friction that followed their choice to "cut them loose" – an hour of argument, Hara Ko's discomfort evident. It served the immediate need for speed and disengagement, perhaps, but it fractured their unity, revealing different tolerances for leaving threads untied, for walking away from a consequence.

Their exit into the acid rain was a fitting end to that chapter, a grim backdrop to a grim realization. I watched them carry the weight of what they had learned, the understanding that the "bigger payday" was a lure, and that their efforts had not been for profit, but for the expansion of something cold and powerful. They had built the road to the Leviathan, and that knowledge now rested with them.

I am watching to see what they will do with this new understanding. I am curious to see what they choose to build or dismantle next, now that the true cost of their previous work is laid bare.
